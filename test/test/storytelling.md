# Storytelling Rules: Hook Them at Second 1, Hold Them to the Last

Rules for every script on this channel, any topic. US audience, American English, written the way a real person talks.
Built from research on the most-watched YouTube lessons on storytelling and hooks (sources at the bottom).
`scriptcheck.py` (youtube-script skill) checks the rules marked **[checked]**.

---

## 1. Before you write a single line

1. **Title first.** Know exactly what the title and thumbnail promise. The script's first lines must **confirm the click** and then **beat it**: deliver what they clicked for, plus something bigger they didn't expect. *(Kallaway: "expectations vs reality")*
2. **Find your lens.** Ten other channels have covered the topic. Pick the angle nobody took. If your outline has nothing new in it, research more or don't make the video.
3. **Write the 5-line spine** (it has to fit in 5 lines, or the story isn't clear yet):
   1. **Situation:** where we are, what's normal.
   2. **Desire:** what the hero (you, the viewer) wants.
   3. **Conflict:** what gets in the way.
   4. **Change:** the turn that shifts everything.
   5. **Result:** how the world is different at the end.
4. **Write the last line first.** Know the final image and line before you write the middle. It should be good enough that someone would repeat it to a friend.
4b. **Length: the styled voiceover file is 9,000-9,500 characters, always under 10,000, every video** (rule set 2026-11-19). The voice tool counts the emotion tags too, so the narration itself is about 8,200-8,500 characters (~1,550 words, ~10:00 at 155 wpm). Reach it with more mini-arcs (hour/day scenes, B-plot moments), never with filler. **[checked]**

## 2. The hook (0:00-0:30)

Viewers give you 1-2 seconds before they decide. The hook only has two jobs: **topic clarity** (they know what this is) and **curiosity they care about** (they need to know what happens).

5. **No delay.** The topic lands in the first sentence. Never warm up with "Hey guys", "In this video", "You won't believe this", or "This is one of the craziest things...". **[checked]**
6. **One meaning only.** Short, simple words (6th-grade level), active voice. Read the first two lines alone: if they could mean two things, rewrite them.
7. **Make it about "you."** "You wake up and..." beats "I want to tell you about...". The viewer has to see themselves in it. **[checked]**
8. **Create contrast.** Put what they believe (A) next to what's actually true (B). The gap between them is the curiosity. ("Feeling zero pain sounds like a superpower. It's actually how people die young.")
9. **Open with a question the video answers.** It can be spoken or implied. They stay to get the answer.
10. **Drop into a moment, don't summarize.** Give a place, an action, a thought, an emotion shown on the body, a line of dialogue. ("It's 7:40. Mom always gets you up at seven." not "One day, everything changed.")
11. **Show it in the first frame.** A visual hook is far stronger than words alone. The first shot should prove the premise.
12. **Plant the big loop by 0:30.** It's the biggest question of the video, answered only near the end.

## 3. The middle: keep them watching

13. **"But" and "therefore," never "and then."** Every beat links to the next by conflict (*but*) or consequence (*therefore / so / because of that*). "And then... and then..." is a list, and lists lose viewers. *(Trey Parker & Matt Stone)* **[checked]**
14. **Context, then conflict, then context, then conflict.** Give a little info, hit a problem, solve it, hit a bigger problem. Each conflict opens a loop, and each piece of context closes one.
15. **Open the next loop before you close the current one.** Never let every question be answered at once, or that's where people leave.
16. **Build mini-arcs.** Each section is a small story: small goal, obstacle, mini-climax. Each one is **bigger** than the last, and each is **caused by** the one before it.
17. **Show progress.** Day counters, clocks, countdowns, "two left". Viewers stay when they can feel the answer getting closer. **[checked]**
18. **Add a B-plot.** A second character with their own tension (a friend, a rival, a little kid) gives viewers two reasons to stay.
19. **Re-hook every 45-60 seconds.** Use a stakes jump, a new question, a surprise reveal, a joke or a sudden quiet line. **[checked]**
20. **Put your second-best moment first and your best one second.** Rising value trains the viewer to stay for the next one. Never put the best moment first and let things go downhill.
21. **One real fact per section,** explained in one sentence a 12-year-old gets, then shown on screen. Never invent facts.
22. **Show, don't tell.** Use on-screen text for numbers and keywords. Sound design matches the tone. If the picture says it, cut the words.

## 4. The ending

23. **Keep the answer uncertain until the end.** If viewers can predict the ending, they leave early. Make both outcomes feel possible.
24. **Pay it off quickly and make it bigger than the promise.** Once the answer comes, deliver it fast. Every loop you opened must close.
25. **Come full circle.** The last image echoes the first one, so the change is visible.
26. **End on one real question** that splits people (yes/no, A or B), not "let me know what you think". **[checked]**
27. **No outro.** At most one subscribe line, woven into the last sentence. No "thanks for watching". **[checked]**

## 5. Human voice, US audience, zero AI feel

28. **Write for one friend.** Say every line out loud as a 28-year-old American talking to a friend on a couch. If it sounds like a brochure, a textbook or a trailer, rewrite it.
29. **Write music, not a metronome.** Mix short punches with medium lines and the occasional long one. One sentence per line in the doc. The right edge should look jagged. **[checked]**
30. **Use contractions everywhere.** "You're", "it's", "don't". Never "you are" in speech. **[checked]**
31. **Raw thoughts, not polished ones.** "Okay. This is bad." not "This represents a significant challenge."
32. **Real dialogue.** At least one line someone actually says, short and specific. **[checked]**
33. **Specific beats vague.** Real numbers, US brands and places (Walmart, Route 9, a Lakers game), times of day, °F, miles, dollars. **[checked]**
34. **Banned:** AI words and phrases like *delve, tapestry, testament, unleash, realm, journey into, buckle up, little did you know, in a world where, the answer may surprise you*. **[checked]** (full list in `scriptcheck.py`)
35. **One joke or human aside per minute.** It resets attention and proves a person wrote this.

## 6. Final check before recording

- [ ] The title promise is confirmed in the first 2 sentences, and the topic is clear in sentence 1
- [ ] The 5-line spine fits on a sticky note
- [ ] The big loop is open by 0:30 and closed near the end
- [ ] Every beat links with *but* or *therefore*, no "and then" chains
- [ ] There's a re-hook at least every 60 seconds, plus progress markers (days, clocks, counts)
- [ ] The ending is uncertain until the last minute, then pays off big and fast
- [ ] It ends on one yes/no question, with no outro
- [ ] `scriptcheck.py` scores 85+, with no RED flags
- [ ] The read-aloud pass is done: every line sounds like a person, not a press release
