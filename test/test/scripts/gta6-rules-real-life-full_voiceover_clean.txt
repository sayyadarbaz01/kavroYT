So what if GTA 6 rules were real life?

"You look weird today," she says, without looking up.

But the rules come with fine print, and you learn it the hard way.

Rule one: your health comes back on its own. But only halfway. That's exactly how it works in GTA 5.

On Tuesday, you trip down the school stairs and break your arm. Ten seconds later, it's half healed. The other half is the real-world part, because a broken bone takes six to eight weeks.

So you get a cast on an arm that's only half broken. The doctor just writes three question marks on your chart.

Rule two: if you die, you respawn. You wake up outside the closest hospital like nothing happened.

That's how you met the bus. You were chasing an ice cream truck. Don't ask.

In real life, there's no respawn. Your brain starts dying after about five minutes without oxygen. But you walk out those hospital doors two minutes later at full health.

So what's the catch? In GTA, the hospital charges you up to five grand. In real life, they bill you anyway, and the average hospital stay costs over ten thousand dollars.

Rule three is the one you love. Break a law, and you get stars. One star, one cop. Five stars, helicopters.

But get out of their sight long enough, and they forget you. Just like in the game.

Remember that rule, because you didn't read the fine print on this one either.

On Wednesday, you skip school to test it. That night, Dev texts you.

So how real is any of this? Weirdly, rule three is the most real one.

In 1885, a German scientist named Hermann Ebbinghaus spent years memorizing nonsense words and timing how fast he lost them. Without a second look, most of it was gone within a day. It's called the forgetting curve.

Out of sight really is out of mind.

Real cops don't rely on memory, though. A lot of US cities have cameras that read license plates automatically, so in the real world, hiding doesn't last.

But in your world, it does. And you're about to find out how far "they" goes.

Rule three never said the cops forget you. It said "they" forget you. Everyone who lost sight of you.

Dev, after one day. Your teachers. And now Mom, after three.
