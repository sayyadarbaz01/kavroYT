[curious] Here's a real number. The US Foreign Service Institute estimates it takes about eighty-eight weeks of full-time study, around twenty-two hundred classroom hours, for an English speaker to reach working fluency in Japanese. In a time loop, that's just a lot of Tuesdays.

You start noticing other people. The guy whose bike chain snaps at eleven oh two. The woman who drops her phone at three forty. You fix the bike. You return the phone. Every single loop.

[warm] On loop three hundred, you stop trying to impress her, and you just sit on the bench at sunset. Not too close. You don't say anything clever. You say, "Long day, huh?"

[softly] "You have no idea," she says. And that's the most real thing she's said to you in three hundred loops.

So you go back. Every loop. Some days you talk for an hour. Some days you just watch the sky. She never mentions the day before, so you assume she can't remember. But every day, she seems a tiny bit less tired.

By loop four hundred, you know that song by heart. You've stopped hating it. You hum it.

[mysterious] And on the very next loop, humming that terrible song changes everything.

[suspenseful] On loop four hundred and one, the song starts at nine fourteen, and someone behind you is humming it too.

[shocked] It's Leah. And she knows every word.

"You know this song," she says.

"It's... catchy."

[serious] "No, it isn't. It's terrible. Nobody knows it. It came out on a Tuesday three weeks ago, and nobody played it again." She looks at you. "Except at nine fourteen. Every day."

[whispers] "How many loops are you on?" she asks.

"Four hundred and one."

[sad] [long pause] "I'm on one thousand and six."

[amazed] Leah has been stuck in this day six hundred loops longer than you. That's why she knew your coffee order. She's watched you order it more than a thousand times.

[excited] Now there are two of you. And it's the best fifty days of your life.

You break into the rooftop observatory. You eat at every restaurant in town, in one day, fifty times. You teach her guitar. She teaches you the Japanese you got wrong.
