[tense] And then, in the library, you see the one number you really didn't want to see.

[softly] Then you see Maya. Third row of the library, same seat every Thursday. ... Thirty-one thousand, two hundred and seven.

Out of eight billion people, she's in the top thirty-two thousand. You are in the bottom billion. [deadpan] If the ranking means anything, she is way out of your league.

[excited] "Bro. Go talk to her."

[whispers] "She's thirty-one thousand."

"I don't know what that means, but it sounds like a lot of thousands. Go."

You walk over. On the way, a freshman drops her pen, and you hand it back without thinking. [curious] And then you notice something strange. Your number twitches. Just a few digits. ... It drops by twenty-two.

"Hi. You're in my Econ section, right?"

You say the smoothest thing you've ever said in your life.

[nervous] "Yes. Section. Econ. Me."

[excited] That twitch changes everything. If the number can move, you can move it. So you try.

You go to the gym. [deadpan] Nothing.

You get an A on your essay. [deadpan] Nothing.

You spend half your savings on sneakers. [deadpan] Nothing. Dev claps anyway.

Then, on the way out of the dining hall, you hold the door for a crowd of people carrying trays. [excited] And the number drops by almost six thousand.

You hold the door again. Nothing. The crowd is gone. [serious] It only counted when it actually helped someone.

You run back to Dev.

[excited] "It moved. It actually moved."

"What did you do?"

"I held a door."

"For who?"

"Like, twelve people with trays."

Dev thinks about this for exactly one second, then sprints to the dining hall and holds the door open for nobody, for ten minutes, while people walk around him through the other door.

[deadpan] His number doesn't move. Not even a little.

"It's broken," he says.

[serious] It's not broken. You just don't understand it yet.
