## 2. The winning formula

### A. Extreme weather beats zombies by ~10×
| Lane | Best result |
|---|---|
| **Extreme temperature** (−100°C cold, 173°F heat) | **1.67M, 715K** |
| Zombie + isolated location (lighthouse, London Tube) | 11–82K |

The two biggest videos have **a single scary number** in the hook and thumbnail: **−100°C** and **173°F**. Same lesson as Miko (a number above heads) and Milo ($1 per second): **one big number = clicks.**

### B. Title pattern
**`[Series Name]: [Time] Survival in a [Place]`**
- "Absolute Zero: 100 Hours Survival in a Frozen City"
- "Boiling Point: 5 Days Survival"
- A memorable two-word series name (a brand), then a time limit, then a place.

### C. Thumbnail pattern (the hits)
- **Warm vs cold split:** a cozy orange-lit room (stove, candle, character in a scarf) next to a freezing blue city outside. Or a cold blue cellar vs a burning red stairway.
- **Huge temperature number** top-right ("−100°C" / "173°F"), with the series name underneath.
- The character is small, calm and cozy, not screaming. It feels like a **movie poster**, not clickbait.
- Zombie-lighthouse thumbnails are darker and busier, and did worse.

### D. Script structure (from chapters)
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | "What if the temperature dropped to −100°C tonight?" + 3 shocking facts (steel breaks like glass, diesel turns to wax, trees explode) |
| 0:30 | **Hour 0 / Day 1**: the disaster hits |
| Every ~40–70 s | A new **hour/day timestamp chapter**: a new problem (pipe bursts, power dies, carbon monoxide, water runs out) and a survival **rule** |
| Middle | A threat besides the weather ("Knocks on the pipe", "Scratching", "Something doesn't breathe") = a hidden zombie/horror layer |
| End | Hour 100 / Day 5 + a choice: "Would you stay with the stove, or go down to the metro?" |

- **14–17 minutes**, longer than Miko/Milo, because survival countdowns hold attention.
- **Description lists the real science** ("This is a fictional story. The science in it is real:" + 5–6 bullet facts).
- Strong SEO keywords (absolute zero, −100 degrees, frozen city, extreme cold survival, stickman animation).

### E. Sequels: watch out
Absolute Zero sequels **decayed**: 1.67M → 233K → 163K → 92K. But the next *new* extreme (Boiling Point) did 715K.
**Lesson:** one sequel is fine; after that, launch a **new extreme** instead of Part 4.
(Milo's sequel *grew* because it escalated the number 1,000×. Headlamp's sequels just continued the story.)

### F. Cadence
About 2–3 long videos a week in week 1, then ~1–2 a week, plus 1–3 Shorts daily (which didn't help).

## 4. What this means for Kip (your Kip Survives series)

Your calendar already has **"Kip Survives: 100 Days in a Frozen Zombie City" (Dec 12)**, which is almost exactly *Absolute Zero*. **Change it** so it doesn't look copied, and lead with an **extreme-weather number** instead of zombies.

**Original extreme-weather topics (not done by Headlamp Guy yet):**
1. **What If It Snowed for 30 Days Straight?** (winter/Christmas timing; snow buries the house to the roof)
2. **What If the Sun Went Dark for 7 Days?** (cold + total darkness; Kip's watch is the only clock)
3. **What If It Rained for 40 Days?** (flood, rooftop survival, water everywhere but none to drink)
4. **What If a Sandstorm Buried Your City for 10 Days?** (ties to Dune: Part Three on Dec 18; replaces the "desert planet" idea)
5. **What If Earth's Air Lost Half Its Oxygen for 72 Hours?**

**Rules to copy (the format, not the content):**
- Title: **`Kip Survives: [Time] in [Extreme Place]`**, or "What If It ___ for [Time]?"
- One huge number on the thumbnail (−60°F, 40 DAYS, 0% LIGHT…) + a warm-vs-cold or safe-vs-danger split.
- Hour/day chapter every ~60 s, each with a new problem + a survival rule.
- 12–16 min. "The story is fictional; the science is real" + bullet facts in the description.
- One sequel max if it hits; then a new extreme.
- Don't rely on Shorts for growth; put the effort into long videos.
- Avoid: the "Absolute Zero" / "Boiling Point" names, −100°C, 173°F, a headlamp mascot, lighthouses.
