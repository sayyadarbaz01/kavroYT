# Launch plan: new English "What If" channel
2026-11-21 · packaging and schedule for the three chosen launch videos · evidence: `reports/viral-whatif/audit-conchaser.md`, `reports/viral-whatif/audit-hypo-room.md`, `reports/viral-ai/top-3-script-niches-2026-11-21.md`, `data/viral-whatif/all_titles.json` (511 titles, 33 new what-if channels). No new data was collected.

## Assumptions (none of these were given in the brief)
- **Audience:** English-speaking viewers aged roughly 16 to 30, worldwide, with US evenings as the main time zone. Every character in every video is an adult (18 or older). Stories are set at college or university, at work, or in adult life. Nothing is set in a high school.
- **Goal and call to action:** the user's goals are 1M+ combined views on the first three videos and YPP thresholds as fast as possible. The only call to action is "subscribe". There is no product, offer or outside link.
- **Capacity:** one long video a day, plus shorts cut from finished long videos. The brief's daily schedule implies this rate. Claude writes the scripts, a TTS voice narrates them, and the visuals are flat 2D silhouette animation.
- **Dates:** "Day 1" is launch day, whenever that is. All times are UTC.
- **Script details are not known yet.** The scripts are being written separately, so any rank number, loop count or scene used in a title or thumbnail is shown as a placeholder. It must be replaced with what the final script actually contains, or that option must be dropped.
- **Channel name:** If Blaze, handle @IfBlaze. Handles and names were checked on YouTube on 2026-11-21. See Channel identity.
- **No Studio data exists yet.** The thresholds in the 48-hour and 7-day checks are starting rules, to be recalibrated once the channel has its own numbers.

## The plan in ten lines
1. Name the channel **If Blaze** (@IfBlaze), and run four pillars that each match a premise family the data shows working: See Everyone's X, Never Existed, Loops and Restarts, and Day-Counter With Someone.
2. On Day 1, publish all three launch videos four hours apart: Rank at 13:00, Mirrors at 17:00, Time Loop at 21:00.
3. Package each one with the proven visual system: flat 2D black silhouettes with white oval eyes, a glowing label over the head, and a 2 to 4 word caption. Use our own lead colours. No suggestive framing.
4. Lead titles: "What If You Could See Everyone's Rank?", "What If Humans Never Invented Mirrors?", "What If You and Your Crush Were Stuck in a Time Loop?"
5. From Day 2, post one long video a day at 16:00. The 14-day calendar rotates the four pillars and never runs the same pillar two days in a row.
6. Shorts are word-for-word cut-downs of the long videos, three per launch video, posted on Days 2 to 6. Each ends unresolved and links to the full story.
7. Run the 48-hour check on Day 3 and the 7-day check on Day 8. Change the thumbnail first, the title second, and only one change per 48 hours.
8. If a launch video breaks out, its follow-up keeps the frame and changes the variable. It is never a reworded remake (ConChaser's remakes went 0 for 3).
9. Subscribers, not watch hours, are the harder YPP threshold. 1,000 subscribers takes about 320,000 long-form views at the median rate on comparable channels, while 4,000 hours takes about 60,000.
10. Rules: fact-check every claim, vary premises and scripts, keep minors out of any romantic context, and never use a thumbnail the video does not deliver.

## Honest expectations
- **The 1M goal is a stretch.** Among the winning new what-if channels, **0 of 32 had all three of their first videos over 1M** and **9 of 32 had one of their first three over 1M**. The realistic route to 1M combined is one launch video breaking out while the other two do moderately. Plan for that, not for three hits.
- **Subscribers are the slower threshold.** Subscribers per lifetime view on 13 new what-if channels range from 0.13% (What1fy) to 0.75% (Gus Maybe), with a median of 0.31% (ConChaser 0.29%, Hypo Room 0.54%). At the median, 1,000 subscribers takes about **320,000 views**, and the range is roughly 133,000 to 750,000.
- **Watch hours come sooner.** 4,000 public watch hours is 240,000 minutes. At a 4-minute average view duration that is about **60,000 long-form views**, or about 80,000 at 3 minutes. Shorts views do not count toward the 4,000 hours. The shorts-only route (10M shorts views in 90 days) is out of reach on this evidence: Hypo Room's shorts have a median of 9,600 views.
- **Review takes time after the thresholds.** YPP review typically takes about a month after the thresholds are met. Reviewers look at the channel's main theme, its most-viewed and newest videos, and its metadata. That is why the variation rules at the end matter.
- **No numbers are promised.** The evidence is public data from channels 2 to 4 weeks old. It shows associations, not causes.

### Rule for a follow-up to a launch video that breaks out
**What counts as a breakout:** at the 48-hour check, a launch video has at least 3x the views of the other two, or at the 7-day check at least 2x the channel median, with Browse plus Suggested above 50% of its traffic.

**Do:** keep the frame and change the variable. Script it within 24 hours. It takes the next slot not yet in production, and the displaced title moves to Day 13 or 14.

| Breakout | Follow-up (keeps the frame, new variable) |
|---|---|
| Rank | Bring the next pillar-1 stat forward (Future Salary is already Day 2, so then Future Job or Mental Age), and add a new stat to Day 13: "See How Many People Remember You?" |
| Mirrors | Bring "Humans Never Invented Money?" forward, and add a new never-invented object with a social stake (for example "What If Humans Never Invented Names?"). Keep one dating or status room in it. |

## Measurement

### 48-hour check (Day 3, for the three launch videos)
In YouTube Studio, for each launch video:
1. **Impressions and click-through rate**, split by traffic source (Browse, Suggested, Search). YouTube says half of all channels and videos have an impressions CTR between 2% and 10%. Compare the three launch videos against each other first.
2. **Audience retention**: the percentage still watching at 0:30 (the first-30-second drop), and where the steepest drops happen.
3. **Average view duration**, and the share of views from Browse plus Suggested. That share is the signal of a breakout.
4. **Subscribers gained** per 1,000 views. Compare it with the 0.31% median on comparable channels (3.1 per 1,000).

**When to swap a thumbnail:**
- Decide only after at least 1,000 Browse plus Suggested impressions.
- If the 30-second retention is fine (70% or more still watching) but CTR is below 4% and at least 1.5 points below the best launch video, swap to thumbnail concept B. Use Test & compare if the channel has it.
- If CTR is fine but more than 40% have left by 0:30, the hook is the problem, not the packaging. Do not make the packaging more aggressive. Check that the first 30 s shows what the thumbnail shows, and fix the next script.

**When to swap a title:** only if CTR is still low 48 hours after the thumbnail swap. Change it to the next starred title, and only to a title the video pays off.

**General rules:**
- One change per video per 48 hours.
- Never change a video whose daily views are still rising.
- Reorder the shorts so the leader's cut-downs go first.

### 7-day check (Day 8)
1. **Views per launch video and channel median.** Apply the breakout rule.
2. **Subscribers and watch hours**, against the YPP thresholds (watch hours are shown in the Monetization tab). Project the date for each threshold at the current daily rate.
3. **Retention curves for all videos so far.** Find which script beats lose viewers (for example a room that runs long, or a slow rewind), and send the timestamps to the script team.
4. **Pillar results.** Use the median views of each pillar to weight weeks 3 and 4.
5. **Shorts.** Compare short views with long-video views. Hypo Room's shorts earned about a tenth as much per subscriber. If shorts are under 0.1x of the long-form median, keep them as a byproduct and do not add more.
6. **Comments.** Look for logic complaints (ConChaser's top Future Wealth comment, 403 likes, was "So many flaws with the logic") and fact corrections. Fix factual errors in the description and pin the correction.

## Rules
1. **Label altered or synthetic content when it is realistic.** Set Studio's "altered or synthetic content" answer to Yes whenever a video contains realistic-looking people, places, events or voices that could be mistaken for real. Flat 2D silhouette animation is not realistic. Keep the AI line in every description regardless.
2. **Fact-check every factual claim.** Claude delivers a source list with each script, and a person checks every fact before the voice-over is recorded. Only checked facts go in the description's "Real facts" block. Claude can state wrong facts confidently.
3. **Avoid the repetitious or mass-produced pattern.** Every video needs:
   - a new premise variable
   - a freshly written script, not a find-and-replace of the template
   - its own jokes and twist
   - a different setting or cast mix from the day before.
   Rotate the four pillars and never run one twice in a row. Reuse the beat structures (the evidence supports them), but never reuse sentences or scenes across videos. Recurring characters are fine and help continuity. Identical stories with new labels are not allowed.
4. **No minors in any romantic or suggestive context.**
   - All characters are adults in adult settings: college, university, work.
   - Romantic storylines happen only between clearly adult characters.
   - No high-school settings for romance or attraction premises.
   - Character designs have adult proportions and clothing.
   The Hypo Room audit flags a suggestive thumbnail on a video whose protagonist is 15 as a child-safety risk.
5. **No thumbnail that misrepresents the video.** Every thumbnail scene, number and caption must appear in the video, preferably in the first two minutes. No censor bars, swimwear framing or suggestive captions. Every number in a title or thumbnail matches the script.
6. **No trend, debate or meta pegs.** Hypo Room's Man or Bear (0.11x), GTA 6 Streamer (0.12x) and "This Video Has 25,442 Views" (0.35x) all flopped, and they also age quickly.
7. **This plan does not upload, schedule or post anything.** A person does all of that in Studio.

## Limits
- All evidence is public data from channels 2 to 5 weeks old. CTR, retention, impressions and subscriber history are not public, so the measurement thresholds are starting rules, not benchmarks from these channels.
- The title-pattern medians (length, "What If You", CAPS) are associations across 33 channels and are confounded by channel. The CAPS lift is concentrated in a few channels, so test it rather than assume it.
- The subscribers-per-view ratios are lifetime figures from the niche report table and assume this channel converts like its peers.
- The channel name was checked against the top YouTube search results only, so a small channel could still share the display name. A competitor could publish any of these premises before Day 1. Run a quick YouTube search on each launch title that morning.
- Thumbnails and titles marked "condition" depend on script details that were not available when this plan was written.
