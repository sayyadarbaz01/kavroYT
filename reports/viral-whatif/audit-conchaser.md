## Verdict
ConChaser hit hard in its first week. Five of its first seven uploads (Oct 23 to Oct 29) cleared 2x the channel median, and the IQ and Future Wealth videos (2.85M and 2.37M views) account for 46% of the channel's 11.26M views. The biggest lever is the premise "What If You Could See [a stat] above everyone's head": 17 videos use that title and 10 of them are outliers, while the 11 videos with other premises have no outliers and a median of 0.52x. The biggest risk is that the channel is drifting away from that premise. Since Nov 11 it has mostly made third-person "Alex" gimmick stories and remakes of its own hits, and the median of the newest ten uploads is 83% below the oldest ten. Part of that gap is because the newest videos are younger, but not all of it.

## Area review

**Positioning: clear in the titles, vague in the description.** Every title is "What If..." (27 of 28) or "Imagine If..." (1). The channel description ("Impossible abilities, alternate realities...") does not mention the school and life stories built around a visible stat, which is what the channel actually makes. The last 10 titles mix stat powers, gimmicks, a dating simulator, and a remake, so the promise is getting blurrier.

**Topics: status stats win, gimmicks drag.** Intelligence, money, height, and attraction carry the channel. Reverse exposure (0.52x, 0.54x, 0.23x), trigger-and-reward mechanics (1.77x, 0.66x, 0.27x, 0.16x), and world swaps (1.16x, 0.52x, 0.32x) do not produce outliers. See the premise-family list below.

**Packaging: "What If You Could See Everyone's X?" with a glowing number over a head.** Title length does not separate winners from losers: outliers have a median of 47.5 characters and underperformers 48. The difference is in the structure and the stat. Thumbnails reuse the same lead character, a round black head with big white eyes in a yellow hoodie, set against a rival whose label contrasts with his.

**Hooks: the title pays off almost immediately.** IQ shows the floating "70 IQ" at 19 to 22 s and the narrator's own 160 at 25 s. Future Wealth shows $8.7B over a homeless man at 6 to 8 s. Future Height shows the two height numbers at 22 to 28 s. Attractive shows the YES/NO label at 25 to 31 s. The top four all open in the middle of a scene with the narrator losing to a rival, then rewind ("let me explain", "this morning"). The weakest video delays the power to 114 s.

**Format and length: long-form only, 13 to 15 minutes, about 150 wpm.** The 780 to 899 s range has a median of 2.33x. Narration speed barely differs between outliers (median 152.9 wpm) and underperformers (145.2 wpm). There are no shorts. The channel has never tested whether shorts could feed the long-form videos.

**Cadence: one video a day, very consistent.** The channel posted 28 videos in 29 days with one missed day (Nov 10). The schedule is not the problem. What changed is quality and premise choice. The comment "Congrats on 10000 subscribers" on the Future Height video (posted Oct 31) suggests subscribers roughly tripled in three weeks. That comes from a comment, not a measurement.

**SEO hygiene: weak and inconsistent.** 26 of 28 videos have no tags. 11 of 28 have chapters, and only one outlier (IQ) does. The first video's description (BepbYfGBO5I) describes a different film ("stick figure... cup trick in various urban settings... weekly animation breakdowns"), so it looks like an unedited auto-generated description. No description has links, and none points to playlists or related videos. Descriptions follow a pattern: the title as a question, a one-line teaser with the contrast numbers, a stakes line, a question to the viewer, and from Nov 5 on, `#WhatIf #Animation #ConChaser`. The hashtags made no visible difference.

**Audience: young, school-age, and they like twists with a moral.** Praise goes to the twists and the moral (for example: "The Daniel twist really got me... the number was never a destiny" on Future Wealth, and "This story hits so different" with 50 likes on IQ). Top complaints are about logic ("So many flaws with the logic of this video", 403 likes, the top-liked comment on Future Wealth; "70iq is basically mentally disabled, oliver cant be like that", 30 likes) and animation glitches ("why near the end is jake invisable", 104 likes; "The mug is floating the table is Invincible", 15 likes; "walk animations"). Viewers suspect AI production ("is this completely made with claude opus 5.5 or any other tool?", 94 likes; "AI IS GETTING GOOD"; "How these videos are made?"). Requests include "What if you could read everyone's mind". There are also signs of competition: "I... uploaded a video with the exact same title around 36 hours ago" (27 likes, IQ). Several comments are long, generic praise, which may or may not be organic. I cannot tell from public data.

### The template both videos share (for writing scripts with Claude)

1. **Cold open, 0 to 35 s.** Open in the middle of a humiliation or contrast scene. Show the stat over a rival or stranger, then the narrator's own stat in a mirror or window. The two numbers contradict status (a 70 IQ kid gets the A+, a homeless man is a future billionaire). End with a line that opens a loop.
2. **Rewind:** "Let me explain" or "This morning...".
3. **Discovery montage, about 35 s to 2:00.** The family's numbers, a gag that the dog has no number, a street survey of 3 to 5 joke readings (both videos use a parking-meter gag), then a search or deduction that states the rule.
4. **Cast.** A loyal, comic best friend whose low number is played for warmth (Jake), and a rival whose number contradicts his status.
5. **Three escalations,** each with bigger stakes (a test, then a competition or scholarship; or a mall, then a startup, then an investor). Show numbers often: every 13 to 50 s.
6. **Midpoint twist at 45 to 56%.** Either the rule turns out to be wrong ("predictions, not destiny") or the rival turns out to be sympathetic (a father's pressure).
7. **Climax:** a public moment where the narrator chooses character over the number.
8. **Callback:** the numbers are still there but no longer matter; one moral sentence; one comic button from the sidekick.
