# Script 3: What If You and Your Crush Were Stuck in a Time Loop?

- **Templates:** Hypo Room day-counter story ("Only Man on an Island of 100 Amazons", 901k), crossed with the reborn and time-loop family, which has the best median of the premise families (151k).
- **Narration:** second person, present tense, one narrator. Character lines are in quotes.
- **Setting:** university, final year. Every character is 21 or older.
- **Length:** about 1,482 spoken words, roughly 10.6 minutes at 140 wpm.
- **Progress bar:** an on-screen loop counter, top left.
- **Planted device:** the café song.
- **Seeded mystery:** Leah's line on Loop 1.
- **Visual cues** are in [brackets].

---

## HOOK (0:00 to 0:42)

[Your alarm reads 7:00 a.m. Counter: LOOP 1.]

You and your crush are stuck in a time loop. Every morning at seven, the same day starts again.

[Leah crossing the quad, earphones in. Close-up of you, frozen.]

That's Leah. You've liked her for three years. Today is the last day of your final semester, and tonight is the graduation party. After that she moves to the other side of the country.

[You open your mouth. A bus drives past, loud. She's gone.]

You were going to tell her today. You didn't.

[Alarm. 7:00 a.m. Counter: LOOP 2.]

And then the day starts again.

By the end of this video you'll find out why Leah already knows your coffee order, what happens on Loop 1,006, and the one thing that finally breaks the loop.

## LOOPS 1 TO 10: PANIC (0:42 to 2:58)

[Counter: LOOP 2. You sit bolt upright.]

On Loop 2 you think it was a dream. Then your roommate Sam says the exact same thing he said yesterday.

"Bro, last day. Last party. Last chance. Tell her or I will."

[Same angle as yesterday. Sam, same hoodie, same cereal spill.]

Same hoodie. Same cereal on the same hoodie.

[Café. A cheesy pop song starts on the radio. You notice it.]

At the campus café the same song comes on the radio at exactly 9:14. Remember that song.

[Leah at the counter. She turns to you.]

Leah is in line ahead of you. On Loop 1 you didn't notice what she said. On Loop 2 you do.

"Oat latte, extra hot, no foam. Right?"

[You stare. You haven't ordered yet.]

You haven't ordered yet. That is your order. You've never told her your order.

[Counter jumps: LOOP 3, LOOP 4, LOOP 5. You trip, spill coffee, get hit by a frisbee.]

Loops 3, 4 and 5 go badly. You trip into her. You spill her coffee. You get hit in the face by a frisbee while saying "Hey Leah."

[Counter: LOOP 5. You, in your room, shaking Sam by the shoulders.]

On Loop 5 you tell Sam everything.

"I'm stuck in a time loop. Today keeps happening. You've said 'last chance, tell her or I will' to me five times."

[Sam, mouth full of cereal, deeply unimpressed.]

"Prove it."

"In ten seconds you're going to spill cereal on your hoodie."

[Sam, very carefully, puts the bowl down on the desk. A pigeon flies into the window. Sam jumps. The cereal goes everywhere.]

"Okay," says Sam, covered in cereal. "Okay. That's creepy. But also, if the day resets, there are zero consequences."

[Counter: LOOP 6, 7, 8. Sam eating an entire cake. Sam riding a shopping trolley down a hill. Sam dyeing his hair green.]

So for the next three loops you let Sam in on it, and he spends them eating an entire cake, riding a shopping trolley down the library hill, and dyeing his hair green. Every morning he wakes up with no memory of it, and you have to explain it all over again.

[Counter: LOOP 9. You at the bus station, buying a ticket to another city.]

On Loop 9 you try to escape. You get on a bus to a city four hours away. If the loop is tied to campus, maybe you can outrun it.

[The bus on a highway at night. 11:59. 12:00. White flash. Alarm. 7:00 a.m. Your dorm room.]

At midnight, on a highway in the middle of nowhere, there's a white flash. And you wake up in your dorm. Loop 10.

You can't outrun it. It's not the place. It's the day.

So if you can't escape the day, you'll have to win it.

## LOOPS 11 TO 50: THE PLAN (2:58 to 4:47)

[Counter: LOOP 11. A whiteboard covered in a timeline of the day.]

On Loop 11 you get organised. Here's what you know. The day always resets at seven. Nothing you do carries over except your memory. And you get unlimited practice.

[Montage. You learn Leah's favourite band, her favourite book, her favourite food.]

So you study. Her favourite band. Her favourite book. The fact that she hates mushrooms, which you learn by giving her mushrooms on Loop 12.

[Counter: LOOP 19. You at the party, delivering a perfect, rehearsed speech.]

By Loop 19 you've written the perfect speech.

"Leah. Since the day we met in first-year Chemistry, I've—"

[Leah, polite but distant.]

"That's... really nice. Did you memorise that?"

[Counter: LOOP 20. You, face in pillow.]

She can tell. It sounds rehearsed because it is.

[Counter rolls: LOOP 21 to LOOP 26.]

You try being funny. Being mysterious. Being casual. You try fifty different versions of yourself, and each one gets the same polite smile.

[Counter: LOOP 27. You in sunglasses and a leather jacket, leaning on a wall.]

Loop 27 is "mysterious". You wear sunglasses indoors and say almost nothing. Leah asks if you're okay and offers you a glass of water.

[Counter: LOOP 33. You holding a guitar you clearly cannot play.]

Loop 33 is "musical". You can't play guitar yet. You play one chord, very confidently, for four minutes.

[Counter: LOOP 41. You "accidentally" bumping into her in five different places in one day.]

Loop 41 is "fate". You accidentally bump into her in five different places in one day. By the fifth one she looks genuinely worried.

"Are you... following me?"

"No. The universe is."

[Counter: LOOP 42. Pillow over face.]

You do not say that again.

[Leah on Loop 50, alone on a bench in the quad at sunset, staring at nothing. You notice for the first time.]

But on Loop 50 you notice something you'd missed. At sunset, Leah sits alone on a bench in the quad. She doesn't look at her phone. She just stares at the sky like she's waiting for something.

Every loop. Same bench. Same face. Like she's tired.

You don't know it yet, but that bench is the most important place in this whole story.

## LOOPS 51 TO 400: GETTING BETTER AT LIFE (4:47 to 6:30)

[Counter: LOOP 51. You quit the whiteboard. You sit in the quad and watch the day happen.]

On Loop 51 you stop trying to win her over and just live the day.

[Montage: you learning guitar, you learning to cook, you learning Japanese from a library book.]

With unlimited Tuesdays, you get good at things. You learn guitar. You learn to cook something that isn't toast. You start learning Japanese from a library book, one day at a time.

[Text card: about 2,200 class hours.]

Here's a real number. The US Foreign Service Institute estimates it takes about 88 weeks of full-time study, around 2,200 classroom hours, for an English speaker to reach working fluency in Japanese. In a time loop, that's just a lot of Tuesdays.

[Counter: LOOP 214. You help an old man carry his groceries. You fix Sam's bike. You return a lost phone.]

You start noticing other people. The guy whose bike chain snaps at 11:02. The woman who drops her phone at 3:40. You fix the bike. You return the phone. Every single loop.

[Counter: LOOP 300. You at the sunset bench. Leah is there. You sit at the other end.]

On Loop 300 you stop trying to impress her, and you just sit on the bench at sunset. Not too close. You don't say anything clever. You say, "Long day, huh?"

[Leah, startled, then a small laugh.]

"You have no idea," she says. And that's the most real thing she's said to you in three hundred loops. 

[Counter: LOOP 301, 302, 303. The bench every day. Short conversations. Small laughs.]

So you go back. Every loop. Some days you talk for an hour. Some days you just watch the sky. She never mentions the day before, so you assume she can't remember. But every day she seems a tiny bit less tired.

[Counter: LOOP 400. You at the café. The song starts at 9:14. You hum along.]

By Loop 400 you know that song by heart. You've stopped hating it. You hum it.

And on the very next loop, humming that terrible song changes everything.

## MIDPOINT TWIST: LOOP 401 (6:30 to 7:18)

[Café, 9:14. The song starts. You hum. Behind you, someone else is humming too.]

On Loop 401 the song starts at 9:14, and someone behind you is humming it too.

[Leah, humming. She stops when she sees you looking.]

It's Leah. And she knows every word.

[Close-up: her face. Quiet.]

"You know this song," she says.

"It's... catchy."

"No, it isn't. It's terrible. Nobody knows it. It came out on a Tuesday three weeks ago and nobody played it again." She looks at you. "Except at 9:14. Every day."

[Long pause. The café noise drops away.]

"How many loops are you on?" she asks.

"Four hundred and one."

[She laughs, but it's a sad laugh.]

"I'm on one thousand and six."

[Counter flickers: LOOP 401 / LOOP 1,006.]

Leah has been stuck in this day six hundred loops longer than you. That's why she knew your coffee order. She's watched you order it more than a thousand times.

## LOOPS 401 TO 450: TWO PEOPLE, ONE DAY (7:18 to 9:06)

[Montage. You and Leah, finally together, doing everything.]

Now there are two of you. And it's the best fifty days of your life.

[You and Leah sneak into the closed rooftop observatory. The sunset.]

You break into the rooftop observatory. You eat at every restaurant in town, in one day, fifty times. You teach her guitar. She teaches you the Japanese you got wrong.

[You and Leah at the café at 9:13, both pointing at the radio and counting down.]

You make a game of the song. At 9:13 you both count down, and when it starts at 9:14 you sing it, loudly and badly, while the whole café stares.

[Leah, laughing, telling you stories from her loops.]

And she tells you about her six hundred loops alone. The loop where she learned to juggle. The loop where she got lost in the woods and made friends with a deer. And the hundred loops in a row where she just slept, because she was too sad to get up.

"Six hundred days of the same Tuesday," she says. "You start to forget what it's like when tomorrow is different."

[Sam, at the party, watching the two of you. Confused.]

Sam is very confused. From his side, you and Leah met this morning and have been finishing each other's sentences all day.

"Since when are you two a thing?"

"About forty days," says Leah.

"It's been one day."

"Sure, Sam."

[Leah, quiet, on the rooftop.]

But one night on the roof she tells you why she thinks it's happening.

"The first loop, I was supposed to tell someone something important. Tonight. At the party. I didn't. And then the day started again."

"Tell who?"

She doesn't answer.

[Counter: LOOP 449. You notice: every loop, at the party, she leaves at exactly 11:58 p.m.]

You notice something you missed for four hundred loops. Every night at the party, at 11:58, she leaves. Two minutes before midnight. Every time.

## CLIMAX: LOOP 450 (9:06 to 10:02)

[The party, 11:55 p.m. Music, lights. Leah by the door, coat on.]

On Loop 450 you don't let her leave.

[You reach the door. She turns.]

"You always go at 11:58. Why?"

[She looks at the floor.]

"Because the first time, at 11:58, I was going to tell you I liked you. And I walked out instead. And I've been too scared to try again, because if I said it and you didn't feel the same, I'd have to live that answer over and over. Forever."

[Close-up: you. The whole thing lands.]

A thousand and six loops. She's been reliving this day because of the exact same thing you have.

[The clock: 11:59.]

You don't have a speech. You threw it away four hundred loops ago.

"I've liked you since first-year Chemistry. You told me my safety goggles were on upside down. I didn't even say thank you. I just walked into a cabinet."

[She laughs. Really laughs.]

"I remember the cabinet."

[The clock: 12:00. Silence. Then: 12:01.]

The clock hits midnight. Then 12:01.

## ENDING (10:02 to 10:24)

[Morning. Alarm. You flinch. The clock reads 7:00 a.m., but the date has changed. Counter: blank.]

You wake up at seven. You flinch. The date has changed. It's Wednesday.

[Your phone buzzes. A message from Leah: "oat latte, extra hot, no foam?"]

Your phone buzzes. It's Leah: "Oat latte, extra hot, no foam?"

[Café. She's waiting with two coffees. At 9:14 the song plays. You both groan.]

And at 9:14 the song comes on. You both groan. It's still terrible.

[Sam in the background, cereal on hoodie, giving a thumbs up.]

Sam still has cereal on his hoodie. Some things don't need a time loop.

## VIEWER QUESTION (10:24 to 10:35)

[Black screen, white text.]

So would you rather be stuck in a time loop with your crush, or never get a second chance at all? Tell me in the comments.

## Production notes

- **Hook:** the title is restated in the first sentence, and the open loop ("By the end of this video") comes at about 0:29, matching the Hypo Room template.
- **Planted device:** the 9:14 song appears at about 1:05, 6:25, 6:30, 7:43 and 10:14, and is paid off at the twist and the ending.
- **Seeded mystery:** the coffee order is planted at about 1:16 and explained at about 7:13.
- **Length:** about 1,482 spoken words, 10.6 minutes at 140 wpm. Times above are estimated from word count.
- **Progress bar:** the loop counter. Hypo Room used a spear count from 0 to 100.
- **Real fact:** one, and it is signposted ("Here's a real number"). Checked online on 2026-11-21: the State Department's FSI page puts Category IV ("super-hard" languages) at 88 weeks, or 2,200 class hours, and Japanese is listed in Category IV. Source: [state.gov, foreign language training (archived)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20210105072258/https://www.state.gov/foreign-language-training). The figure counts class hours only, and FSI students also study on their own, so keep "around".
- **Ages:** all characters are final-year university students, 21 or older. Keep thumbnails non-suggestive.
- **Mid-roll subscribe ask, optional:** if you add one, place it right after "I'm on one thousand and six" (about 7:03), the same spot after a cliffhanger that Hypo Room used.
