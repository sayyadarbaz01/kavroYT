# Channel audit: Rosoo Tiny TV
https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCVWY3gpmIRZUnFv0QdFgyCg · 11,000 subscribers · sampled 18 videos (whole channel), 0 shorts · 2026-11-21

## Verdict

Rosoo Tiny TV reached 4,542,871 views on 18 long videos in about two weeks, but the collector data does not support the "brand-new AI channel went viral" framing: it is a satellite of an established kids-animation network (the flagship "Rosoo Nursery Rhymes" has 3,810,000 subscribers and is credited to VIC Media, the same name in this channel's copyright line), and nothing in the descriptions, tags, or the one transcript obtained mentions AI. The biggest lever is the story-led police / thief / baby-in-danger title on a 16 or 25 minute song compilation, which accounts for 6 of the 7 outliers. The biggest risk for anyone copying it is that the result is concentrated (two videos hold 46% of all views) and probably depends on a pre-built library, proven characters, and network association that a solo newcomer does not have.

## Area review

**Positioning.** Clear from the titles, absent from the channel page. The channel description field is empty and there are no channel tags. Every one of the 18 titles ends in "Rosoo Nursery Rhymes" (17 as a pipe-separated suffix, one as "+ More Rosoo Nursery Rhymes & Kids Songs"), which is the name of the 3,810,000-subscriber flagship channel, not this channel's own name. The video description states the audience: "toddlers and preschoolers".

**Topics.** Police, thieves, and babies in danger outperform (see above). Classic rhymes, Christmas, superhero, and "learn about jobs" framing drag. The same themes dominate the flagship: 5 of its 30 newest titles name "Sheriff Zozo" and many of the rest are police-and-thief stories.

**Packaging.** Winning titles follow "[Character] [does something to] [villain or baby] | [second story or category] | Rosoo Nursery Rhymes". The five thumbnails opened (top 3, bottom 2) share one style: glossy 3D cartoon characters, one frozen action, no text overlay, saturated colours. The top two show a chase or a theft in progress (monkey police officer chasing a clown-faced man; masked thief pulling a trolley away from a baby). The bottom two show calm scenes (a Santa train; a baby eating candy). That is four thumbnails, so it is an observation, not a proven pattern.

**Hooks.** Only one transcript was obtained (C85F9_q80C4). The first sung line arrives at 8 seconds and the first chorus ("it's a zombie ... just a sleepwalking baby") by 19 to 35 seconds. The title's subject does not open the video: the first sheriff lyric is at 479 seconds and the "bad guy" luring children with treats is at 643 seconds of 971. The word "clown" never appears in the captions. So the compilation does not lead with the titled story; viewers get a different song first. Whether that helps or hurts retention cannot be measured from public data.

**Format and length.** Everything is long-form, 10.8 to 30.3 minutes. Durations cluster at two template lengths: 8 videos between 903 and 1,003 seconds (about 15 to 17 minutes) and 5 videos between 1,505 and 1,537 seconds (about 25 minutes). Videos of 20 minutes or more have a median of 205,500 views (n=8) against 75,500 for those under 20 minutes (n=10), but the top video is 16.2 minutes and outliers are split 4 long to 3 short, so length is a weak signal next to topic.

**Cadence.** Four videos carry the first listing date (2026-11-08), then one upload on every listing date from 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-20 and two on 2026-11-21. The collector's 9.15 uploads per week is inflated by the launch batch; after it the rate is about one a day, matching the description line "Subscribe for new videos everyday!".

**SEO hygiene.** Templated. All five descriptions fetched are identical apart from the title line: three hashtags, a subscribe line, the title, two playlist links ("Rosoo Tiny TV" and "Rosoo Nursery Rhymes"), ten more hashtags, a boilerplate welcome paragraph, and "Copyright VIC MEDIA ENTERTAINMENT All Rights Reserved." Each video has 22 or 23 tags, of which 16 are identical across all five; the remaining 6 or 7 are topic tags (for example "police officer song", "baby police", "Sheriff Zozo", "grocery store song"). No chapters on any of the five. Category is Music on all five.

**Audience.** No comment data. The collector returned an empty comment list for all five videos and a null comment count, which is consistent with comments being disabled on made-for-kids videos; the collector does not expose the made-for-kids flag, so that cause is inferred. Like rates on the top three are 0.16%, 0.25%, and 0.16% of views.

## How it is made

Evidence means it is in the saved collector JSON. Inference means it is my reading of that evidence.

**AI tools**

- Evidence: none of the five descriptions, none of the tag lists, and nothing in the one transcript mentions AI, any generator, or any synthetic-content note. The collector does not expose YouTube's altered-content label, so its presence or absence is unknown.
- Evidence: the five thumbnails share a uniform glossy 3D style, and a similar baby character with an orange curl appears in at least three of them (C85F9_q80C4, AxiwLcsxneA, -uETS7ZDi_U).
- Inference: the consistent characters and the studio credit point to a conventional 3D animation pipeline run by a company. AI may be used somewhere (lyrics, vocals, thumbnails), but this audit found no evidence for it. The premise that this is an "AI-generated" channel is unverified.

**Who is behind it**

- Evidence: every description ends "Copyright VIC MEDIA ENTERTAINMENT". The flagship channel "Rosoo Nursery Rhymes" (https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCVrGBHIrHNkeXvzIcy0tt8w) has 3,810,000 subscribers and its description says "By VIC Media". Its 30 newest videos have a median of 174,500 views and it uploads 10.15 times a week.
- Evidence: a search for "Rosoo Nursery Rhymes" returned videos from seven other Rosoo-named channels (Rosoo Kids Songs, Rosoo Kindergarten, Rosoo Sing & Learn, Rosoo - Song For Kids, Rosoo TOWN, Rosoo Police, and, via a second search, Rosoo Happy Time), with flagship videos at 26,410,134, 27,923,312, and 24,255,051 views.
- Evidence: the same jazz stream title that sits on this channel's streams tab appears under at least ten other kids channels in search, including Rosoo Sing & Learn and Rosoo Happy Time.
- Inference: Rosoo Tiny TV is a new satellite channel in an established multi-channel network, not an independent newcomer. Its 122,000 median is in the same range as the flagship's 174,500 median, which fits a network launch far better than an unknown channel going viral. Shared ownership of the other Rosoo-named channels is inferred from the name; only the flagship's VIC Media credit was checked.

**Song and compilation structure**

- Evidence (transcript of C85F9_q80C4, 971 seconds): six songs back to back, each roughly two to two and a half minutes, each a verse and chorus repeated three times, then a "Bye-bye" at 961 seconds. Order: zombie / sleepwalking baby (0 s), police rescue with "meow meow" chorus (137 s), "we can be helpers" kindness song (316 s), "I am the sheriff ... woop woop that's the sound of the police" (479 s), police team chasing a bad guy who offers treats (643 s), "we love going to the beach" (808 s).
- Evidence: the sixth song (beach) has nothing to do with the police title, and the titled story is not the opener.
- Inference: each upload is a compilation assembled from a pool of stand-alone two-to-three minute songs, titled and thumbnailed after one of them, with unrelated songs used as filler. A 25-minute video would hold roughly nine or ten songs at this rate; that was not measured because the other transcripts were unavailable.

**Video length.** Evidence: two template lengths, about 16 minutes (8 videos) and about 25 minutes (5 videos); full range 10.8 to 30.3 minutes.

**Upload cadence.** Evidence: a four-video launch batch, then daily. Inference: launching with four 11-to-23-minute compilations on day one and sustaining one a day is only practical with a finished library.

**Reuse**

- Evidence: the title suffix and a tag carry the flagship's brand; the description links a "Rosoo Nursery Rhymes" playlist; "Sheriff Zozo" is a recurring flagship character; flagship titles cover the same stories (police taking care of baby, thief stories, boo boo song, sharing is caring, Wheels on the Bus).
- Evidence: a search for the top video's title returned a 41-second video with the identical title (U72W3tOeRv4); its channel was not returned.
- Inference: content is very likely recut from the network's existing song library. This was not verified at the footage level; no sibling-channel transcript or video was compared.

## Recommendations

For someone trying to replicate the format, ordered by expected impact.

1. Re-scope the thesis before investing. Evidence: VIC Media credit on both channels, 3,810,000-subscriber flagship, similar medians (122,000 against 174,500). This week: audit the flagship and two other Rosoo-named channels to learn how the network launches satellites, and look for a comparison channel that is demonstrably independent.
2. Build titles around a police, thief, or baby-in-danger event. Evidence: 6 of 7 outliers; 4 of the 6 "Police" or "Sheriff" titles are outliers. This week: write 20 titles in the form "[Character] [action] [villain or baby] | [second hook] | [brand]".
3. Publish compilations, not single songs. Evidence: every video is 10.8 to 30.3 minutes; the one transcript shows six songs in 16 minutes. This week: plan a pool of 12 to 15 stand-alone two-to-three minute songs that can be recombined into 16 and 25 minute cuts.
4. Hold a library before launch, then go daily. Evidence: four uploads on the first date, then one a day. This week: do not publish until at least 10 compilations are finished.
5. Use one recurring cast and one named hero. Evidence: "Sheriff Zozo" leads the top video and five of the flagship's 30 newest; a similar baby character appears in at least three of the five thumbnails opened. This week: lock two or three character designs and a named police character.
6. Thumbnails: one frozen action, no text. Evidence: the two 1,000,000-plus videos show a chase and a theft in progress; none of the five thumbnails carries text. This week: storyboard thumbnails as "villain caught mid-act, baby reacting".
7. Skip emoji, seasonal, and classic-rhyme-led titles at launch. Evidence: all three emoji titles and both Christmas titles are underperformers; no classic-rhyme-led title is an outlier except the vehicle compilation. This week: move classic rhymes to the filler slots inside compilations.
8. Template the metadata and move on. Evidence: descriptions identical apart from the title line; 16 fixed tags plus 6 or 7 topic tags; no chapters. This week: write one description template and a fixed tag block; do not spend time on chapters.

General context, not collector data: mass-produced, repetitive, or reused kids content carries monetisation and policy risk on YouTube. Check the current rules before copying a recut-library model.
